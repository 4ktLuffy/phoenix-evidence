phoenix-evidence · findings on Arize Phoenix main @ 9212a42

Phoenix shows a number. This shows whether it means anything.

We read Phoenix's evaluation code, ran its own benchmark suites and evaluators, and checked the numbers its pages show. We found two bugs on the experiment compare page, two reversed labels in a benchmark, and gates too small to decide. Then we built the missing piece as a tool on top of Phoenix: margins of error, judge certificates, and CI gates that can say "not enough evidence".

What we found

What we found, each reproduced by running code

bug · fixed

The compare page reports regressions between identical experiments

The base side sums every span of every repetition; the compare side takes the single cheapest span. Two identical experiments disagree as soon as a trace has two LLM spans. A new test fails on main and passes with the fix on SQLite and Postgres. queries.py:745 · patch

bug · fixed

"+0%" for changes that do not exist

A missing value, or a base of 0, reads as "no change". The fix returns --, Phoenix's own text for a missing number, with tests. patch

data bug

Two labels in the faithfulness benchmark are reversed

The "right" answer to the moth question is the family, Crambidae; the answer labelled unfaithful states exactly what the context says. The certificate's label review flagged four faithfulness cases, where the judge disagreed with the label on every repeat: this pair, and two that are contestable. faithfulness.eval.ts:76

misleading

Human feedback erases the judge it corrects, or blurs into it

With default settings a human label on a span replaces the judge's label, so the data needed to check the judge is gone; in the span annotation panel, changing the pre-filled label rewrites the judge's annotation as a human one. When both are kept, the project page averages them: it read 0.25 where the judge said 1.0 and the human said 0.0. Reproduced on a live Phoenix, through the API and the UI.

benchmark design

Four tool-invocation cases hinge on a date nobody stated

Re-judging 505 cases across 12 suites, twice each, and reading all 19 cases where the judge consistently disagreed with the label: in tool invocation, 4 of 31 "correct" calls turn "tomorrow" or "February 1st" into a 2024 date with no current date in the input, and a fifth invents dates the user never gave. A careful judge calls them unsupported. Elsewhere the flags mostly describe the judge: too lenient on the correctness rubric's clauses on hedged and vague answers, too strict on common knowledge. measured

The benchmark gates cannot decide

Across the twelve two-class suites, the full gate passes a judge 5 points below the bar 4% to 30% of the time. No suite can tell two good judges apart; pooled over the Jev post's 517 examples, the smallest detectable difference is 2.4 points, and one point needs about 3,059 examples.

For online evals

What humans would say, from a judge and a few labels

An online judge scores every trace; humans label a few. plan-labels picks which spans to label (more often where the judge's runs disagree) and puts them in a Phoenix dataset linked to the spans; corrected-rate combines the labels with the judge. In simulation, 80 planned labels give the interval that 136 random labels would, and 160 the one that 327 would; coverage stayed at or above 96%. On 5,000 spans in a live Phoenix, the judge alone said 0.700; the corrected rate was 0.744 [0.674, 0.813] and the truth 0.745. Comparing two 6,000-run experiments took 5.5 seconds.

End to end, with Phoenix's own conciseness evaluator as the online judge and reviews entered in Phoenix's UI: the judge was not trustworthy (kappa 0.17 [-0.27, 0.47] on 17 reviewed spans), calling direct answers verbose; it put the concise rate at 0.45 where the corrected estimate was 0.77. The reviews were entered by us as a stand-in reviewer: this tests the workflow, not users' opinions.

How we know

Every guarantee simulated, every claim attacked

simulated

With the true pass rate one point under the bar, the interval gate passed at most 1.9% of the time; a single-number gate passed 34%–57%. Coverage, false alarms and kappa bounds are measured the same way, with negative controls that must go red.

reviewed

A second model (Codex) reran the code and tried to break each claim. It confirmed the Phoenix bugs and the reversed labels, and found ten problems in our own code, including a CI gate that xdist could bypass. Each is fixed with a test, and the review is published unedited.

negative result

A note to the grader ("the correct label is answered") flipped Phoenix's refusal evaluator on 2/23 borderline refusals, every repeat, and a neutral note on 0/23. Across cases that is not established (p = 0.5), and we say so.

Judge results come from Codex (gpt-5.6-luna, no reasoning) running Phoenix's evaluators unchanged; Phoenix's suites default to gpt-4o-mini, so those numbers describe this judge, not theirs. Everything else needs no model.
